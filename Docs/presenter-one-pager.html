VitalPower

Presenter one-pager · Caribbean AI Summit hackathon · 9 Oct 2026

In one sentence: when the power goes out in Puerto Rico, we tell responder organizations which patients who depend on electricity to call first, and why.

The problem

  • Some people depend on electricity for medical equipment: oxygen machines, ventilators, CPAP, dialysis, insulin kept in the fridge.
  • Many home oxygen machines have no battery. Portable ones and ventilators last a few hours.
  • There is no complete list. Medicare data counts 44,483 such patients in Puerto Rico; the electric company's list has about 3,000. Nobody knows who to call first.

What we built

  • A list of patients, each one added with their permission.
  • A watcher that reads LUMA's public outage data every 3 minutes.
  • A call list that puts the most urgent patient at the top and says why.

How it works, step by step

1. A caregiver registers a patient by talking or typing.   2. LUMA reports an outage in the patient's zone.   3. The patient or caregiver gets one question: "¿Tiene luz en su casa?"   4. The organization sees a ranked call list.   5. A coordinator takes the case, calls, and writes down what happened.

Three views, one for each kind of user

Patients and caregivers

Address: /app · Spanish

  • Register by voice or text
  • Check the form, give consent, save
  • Answer the outage question with two big buttons or in their own words
  • See their patients and outage status

Organizations

Address: /org · Spanish

  • Ranked call list for their towns only
  • A reason next to every name
  • "Tomar caso": the first organization gets it
  • Read the reply, confirm, get a call script, record the result

Admin (us)

Address: /admin · Spanish

  • Log of every LUMA reading
  • Replay a recorded outage for demos
  • Approve new organizations
  • See patients no organization covers

Who gets called first (fixed rules, no AI)

  • Call now: no power confirmed and 4 hours of battery or less. Also: no answer after 15 minutes from someone on a ventilator, oxygen or CPAP.
  • Call soon: no power confirmed and insulin in the fridge.
  • To confirm: possible outage, nobody has confirmed yet.
  • The reason is shown next to every name, so the order can be trusted.

Where we use AI (Claude)

  • Fills the form from what the caregiver says.
  • Reads the reply to the outage question ("no power, 2 hours of battery").
  • Drafts the call script for the coordinator.
  • A person checks every one before it is used. If the AI is down, people do the step by hand.

Say this: "The AI never decides who goes first. Fixed rules do, and a person confirms every step."

If they ask

Is the data real?
The outage data is LUMA's real public feed. Every patient is made up.
What about privacy?
Patients give consent when they register. Each user only receives what they are allowed to see.
Is there a real login?
Not yet. The demo uses a "Ver como" switcher. Real login is the first thing we would add.
What if LUMA's data stops?
Every screen shows the time of the last reading and warns when it is old.
Two organizations, same patient?
The first one to take the case gets it. The other sees who has it.
How is this different from HHS emPOWER?
Its public map gives counts of Medicare patients by ZIP code. Names go only to health authorities in an emergency. We work any day, for any patient who signs up.
